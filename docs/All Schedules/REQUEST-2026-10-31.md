CarePlan Scheduler — Missouri Complete Service Coverage Project
Regulatory baseline: October 31, 2026 | DHSS/DSDS HCBS

The right goal is to make CarePlan Scheduler capable of recognizing every Missouri DHSS/DSDS HCBS authorization and producing one coordinated participant schedule, regardless of how many different services appear in the care plan.

I verified Missouri's July 2026 Services Units and Rates table and its companion Cost Maximums policy. They establish the official service inventory we should use as our primary coverage baseline.

Nothing in a Fusion care plan is silently discarded. Every authorization must be imported, identified, classified, and either scheduled or explicitly retained as a non-scheduling item.

5. What remains genuinely unresolved
The review covers the published DHSS/DSDS service inventory, but four important points still need authoritative evidence: the actual Appendix 14a/14b Excel formulas and dropdowns, the state's definition of a week for the 49-hour limit, exact Fusion behavior in certain rounding and partial-month scenarios, and a few specialized ILW eligibility/concurrency interpretations.

These are marked as unresolved in the package. Cline can build the verified functionality while retaining a review-required state for those cases, but we should not present unresolved calculations as state-certified.

There is also a wider product boundary: 100% of DHSS's July 2026 rate table is not the same as 100% of Missouri's home healthcare industry. Missouri's Medically Complex Adult, Brain Injury, and DMH waivers, as well as Medicare home health, require their own source-derived rule packs.

6. The standard I would require before approaching Missouri DHSS
21 of 21 services recognized

Every Appendix 1 service is identified, correctly classified, and preserved.


51 of 51 worksheet rows recognized

Exact source labels and task/control relationships are preserved, including medically related household tasks.


One complete participant schedule

Every applicable care task, nursing appointment, transportation event, and other schedulable service appears on one calendar.


Regulatory traceability and transparent validation

Each enforced requirement has a policy source, effective date, test, and documented exception behavior. Unverified rules never silently pass.



These are acceptance targets, not claims that the current app has already passed.

The strongest product positioning is not that CarePlan Scheduler replaces Fusion or Missouri's authorization workbooks. It is that Fusion establishes what care is authorized, and CarePlan Scheduler turns all of those authorizations into one accurate, customizable, operationally useful schedule.

That is a clear value proposition for Missouri providers—and, with demonstrated workbook parity and authoritative rule validation, a credible basis for presenting the application to DHSS.
